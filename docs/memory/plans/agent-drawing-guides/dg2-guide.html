Drawing guide for canvases

Follow it for every chart, report card, slide or post you draw, so your drawings look like the rest of the panel. Pixel values are for the card board; on another board multiply them by its scale, its shorter side / 675 (the figure tile and the sparkline strip use them as they are).

Boards (width x height):
- card 1200 x 675: a chart or report card in a channel (the default).
- slide 1920 x 1080: a slide or a wide report.
- square 1080 x 1080: a social post.
- portrait 1080 x 1350: a 4:5 post.
- story 1080 x 1920: a 9:16 story.
- figure_tile 600 x 360: one figure with its trend.
- sparkline_strip 600 x 120: a name, a figure and a sparkline in one row.

Grid: 12 columns, margin 48, gutter 24, base unit 8; a value axis takes 64 at the left of the plot. Everything is left aligned, never a centred title: the title at the top left, the subtitle under it, the legend under that, the plot in the space left, a source note at the bottom.

Type: one family, font g:Inter (sans when it is not offered). Weights 300, 400, 600, 700, 900 only.
- title: size 44, weight 700, colour ink.
- figure: size 72, weight 700, colour ink.
- subtitle: size 24, weight 400, colour ink2.
- label: size 20, weight 600, colour ink2.
- axis: size 18, weight 400, colour ink3.
- note: size 16, weight 400, colour ink3.
Nothing smaller than 16 on a card board: a channel shows it at half size.

Spacing: multiples of 8 only (8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64). Title to subtitle 8, subtitle to legend 24, legend to plot 40, plot to note 24.

Colours: choose a dark or a light board and keep to its set; give the board its background colour.
- dark: background #14161c, ink #eef0f3 / #a4a9b1 / #6d737c, grid #262930, baseline #3a3e47, series in this order #5892ea, #00ac86, #9580e0, #be8700, #d46d83, other #5d636c, up #3fd0a8, down #f58aa0.
- light: background #ffffff, ink #12151a / #58606a / #8a919b, grid #eceef1, baseline #c9cdd3, series in this order #2f6fed, #0e9a74, #7150e6, #b7791f, #d43d6b, other #a3a9b1, up #0e9a74, down #d43d6b.
Series take their colours in the order given and keep them when a series is filtered out; at most 5 series, the rest folds into other. Text never wears a series colour: titles, labels, values and legends use ink, ink2 and ink3. Up and down colour only a change figure, always with an arrow. Use solid colours, no transparency, so every part stays a layer people can edit.

Marks:
- Bar: at most 40 thick and 56% of its band; the data end rounded 6, square at the baseline; all bars grow from one baseline. 4 between bars of a group; a gap of 2 in the background colour between the parts of a stack; no outlines.
- Line: 3 thick, round joins and caps; a dot of radius 6 at its end with a ring of 3 in the background colour, and the last value beside it.
- Area: the line over a fill of its series colour mixed 14% into the background, as one solid colour.
- Donut: outer radius 190, inner 128, a thin gap between slices, the total in the middle, the legend at the right with each name, count and share.
- Grid and axes: horizontal gridlines only, 1 thick and solid (never dashed), in grid; the baseline 2 thick in baseline; 4 to 5 round steps (0, 10K, 20K).
- Values: label only what the story is about (the highlighted bar, the end of a line), never a number on every point. A legend from two series up; one series needs none, the title says what it is.
- Numbers as the channel's language writes them, with the unit in the subtitle.

Chart kinds: bar, stacked_bar, line, area, donut, sparkline, figure_tile. A bar chart compares amounts, a stacked bar the parts of each amount, a line or an area a change over time, a donut the parts of one whole, a figure tile one number with its change and a sparkline of its trend, a sparkline strip a trend in one row. Two measures of a different scale are two charts, never a second axis.
