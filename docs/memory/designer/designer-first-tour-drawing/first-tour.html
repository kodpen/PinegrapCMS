A content editor’s card

New here? A one-minute tourThree stops on what you can change.
StartNot now
  • Where. 16 px from the window’s right edge, 16 px above the status bar, 320 px wide, over everything but a dialog. The corner is empty at a first visit: nothing is chosen, so the Options column says only “Select an element to edit its properties”.
  • When. Once the page is drawn, and only if no dialog is open, no text is being typed and nothing is dragged; otherwise it waits for the next quiet moment of this visit. Shown once per person and browser, whatever the answer.
  • “Start” begins the tour at its first stop. “Not now” and Escape close the card; the tour stays reachable as “Take the tour” in the Help window (board T4).
  • It takes no focus. The card is announced (“New here? A one-minute tour”) and reached with Tab after the page bar; the canvas keeps the keyboard. From “Start” on, focus goes to each stop’s bubble and back where it was at the end.
  • It comes in with a 160 ms fade and rise of 8 px; with reduced motion it is simply there.
  • Nothing loads for it but the tour’s part, and that only when the card is due: a person who has seen it pays nothing.

T2 · The tour’s five stopsStep FT2. Each stop is a ring round the real control and one sentence in a bubble beside it. The windows are drawn at a third of their size to show where the ring and the bubble stand; under each is its bubble as it is read.

SkipBackNext
  • The ring. 2 px in the accent with a soft 7 px halo, 3 px outside the control’s box, corners 8 px. It follows the control when the window is resized and is drawn again when a panel opens or closes.
  • The bubble. 320 px, beside the ring where there is most room, its arrow on the ring: right of the palette, left of Options, under a button of the page bar, above the status bar. Never over the control it names, never outside the window.
  • Its parts. “Tour · 3 of 5” with five dots, the sentence with its first words in bold, then “Skip” at the left, “Back” and “Next” at the right; the last stop has “Back” and “Done”, the first no “Back”.
  • Nothing is dimmed or locked. The designer stays usable under the tour; a click on the ringed control does what it always does and the tour stays on its stop. The tour waits while a dialog is open and comes back when it closes.
  • A control that is not on the screen (a closed column, a narrow window) is skipped as the model does, and the count says so: “2 of 4”.
  • Ending. “Done”, “Skip” and Escape end it and it is kept as seen. Nothing is shown after it: no summary, no second card.
  • Keys. Focus goes to the bubble’s “Next” at each stop and back to where it was at the end. Enter or the right arrow is Next, the left arrow Back, Escape ends.
  • Read out. Each stop is announced whole: “Tour, 3 of 5. See it. Look at the page as a visitor will, on a phone or a wide screen.”
  • Movement. The ring glides to the next control in 200 ms; with reduced motion it jumps. The ring is drawn over the window, not over the canvas’s page: nothing is added to the page.

  • The canvas stop rings the whole canvas, its bubble inside it at the foot; the parts the editor may change are framed as the designer frames them today, the tour adds no mark of its own.
  • The second stop falls back to the canvas when the Options column is closed, with the same sentence.

TipsOne line the first time you open a feature
  • One window. Title “Help”, 436 px, two parts as a switch under the head. The “?” button opens it on “How do I…”; the ? key and F1 open it on “Keyboard shortcuts”, as today. No second button anywhere.
  • Rows. “Take the tour” first, in the accent, then a line, then the ten tasks, each a row of 36 px with an icon and an arrow. A content editor sees only the tasks they can do: “Change a text or a picture” and “Publish”.
  • A task closes the window and shows its first pointer: the tour’s ring and bubble, headed “How do I · 1 of 3”, with “Close” where the tour has “Skip”. A pointer whose control is not on the screen is skipped.
  • A pointer does not do the step. It shows where; the person presses. Pressing the ringed control moves to the next pointer by itself.
  • “Tips” at the window’s foot, in both parts: a switch, on at first. Off, no first-use line is shown anywhere (board T5); tips already seen stay seen when it is turned on again.
  • Keys. The arrows move over the rows, Enter starts one, the left and right arrows change the part, Escape closes. The switch is the last stop.

History
  • One line. Under the head, across the whole width, on the accent’s soft ground with a line under it: the sentence at 12.5 px, “Got it” at the right. In a narrow panel the sentence wraps and the button stays at the right.
  • Which features. The ones whose plan names a first-use line; each gives its own sentence and a name of its own, kept with what was seen. The tour’s part draws the line; a feature only asks for it.
  • It goes with “Got it”, and by itself once the feature is used for the first time. It never comes back, in any design.
  • Never two at once, never while the tour or a pointer is on the screen, and never over a dialog’s own message.
  • “Tips” off (board T4) shows none; what was seen before stays seen.
  • Read out once when the feature opens; “Got it” is the head’s next Tab stop.

  • The starts. “Add a section” opens the palette on “Blocks”; “Sketch it” starts the Sketch tool; “From a document” opens that feature’s start. Each a card of 184 px with an icon, a name and one line.
  • Drawn by the designer over the canvas, never in the page: nothing of it is saved, published or seen in Preview. It goes the moment the page holds an element, and returns if the page is emptied again.
  • With one start the question is left out: “This page is empty.” and the one card. A content editor sees no starts: an empty page is not theirs to build.
  • Dropping still works. A component dragged from the palette lands on the empty page as today; the starts fade while something is dragged over the canvas.
  • Keys. The cards are Tab stops in their order; Enter or Space starts one. Read out: “This page is empty. How do you want to start? Three choices.”
  • Narrow. Under 620 px of canvas the cards stand one under the other, 250 px wide, the icon at the left.
