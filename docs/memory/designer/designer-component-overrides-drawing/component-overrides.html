Component overrides

The sheet of the plan “Component overrides”: a field of a shared component opened for each place, the place's own values under “This place”, and the component's variants. Sample: the component “Promo card” (Picture, Sale badge, Title, Text, Button) placed twice on “Home”. Every label is in English as the step writes it.

  • Where the row is. Only for an element inside a shared component (opened on its own, or edited from a page) and only for the roles that may edit any component (Administrator, Designer). Elsewhere the menu is as today: “Duplicate”, “Delete”, “Show in Overview”.
  • Which fields. The list holds what the element has of “Text”, “Picture”, “Link”, “Icon”, and always “Shown”. A field bound to data is dimmed with the reason under it.
  • The label. It is the name the field carries in every place. It starts as the element's name; when a second field of the same element is opened both take the field's name after it (“Button text”, “Button link”). An empty label goes back to that default.
  • The mark. A small green sign after the row's label, with the tip “Can change per place: label”. Choosing it opens the same list. No colour alone: the sign is a shape, the tip names it.
  • First use. The line with “Got it” is shown once to a person, the first time a field is opened, and never again.
  • Keyboard. In the menu the arrow keys move, Right or Enter opens the list. In the list Up and Down move between rows, Space flips the switch, Tab goes to “Label”, Escape closes the list and then the menu.
  • Saving. Nothing is sent by itself: the marks are saved with the component, by “Save”. One step of undo takes a switch back.
  • No new button. The feature enters as this one menu row and one row of the command palette; no bar and no panel head gains a button.

  • The section. “Name”, “Content editors” and “Detach” stay as they are today. The list sits between them under the small head “Can change per place”; it is there for the roles that may open a field.
  • Order. The rows are dragged by the grip; with the keyboard Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the focused row. The order here is the order of the rows under “This place”.

Edit the componentPromo card
  • When it shows. Only for a placed component that has an open field. A component with none keeps today's panel, with no “This place” section.
  • A row. The label is the field's label. An empty field shows the component's value as its hint and draws it; typing a value makes it this place's own. A picture opens the picture chooser of today, a link the link field of today.
  • The dot. The 14 px place before the label holds the 7 px dot of a changed row; under the pointer or on focus it becomes the undo arrow with the tip “Reset to the component”. One step of undo. Clearing the field does the same.
  • “Shown”. An open “Shown” is a switch that shows what is drawn here (“Shown” or “Hidden”); the dot tells that this place differs from the component.
  • The head. The count badge holds the number of changed fields and is absent at none; while closed the head shows the first changed text as its summary.
  • The foot. One line and the quiet small button “Edit the component”: it starts the editing of today, for every place (board C4).
  • Refused. A link or picture passes the check its own field passes; a refused value keeps the rose field and the field's own message, and is not kept.
  • Busy. There is no busy state: nothing here calls the server by itself, the values are saved with the page.
  • Keyboard. Tab walks dot, field, dot, field; Enter or Space on the dot resets the row; Escape leaves a field with what it held.

Detach
  • The outlines. The place keeps the green outline of a shared component. While it is selected its open elements carry a thin dashed green outline; nothing is drawn when it is not selected, and nothing in the preview or on the site.
  • Typing. A double click or Enter on an open text starts typing in place; the name tag reads “label · This place”. Escape leaves with what was there, a click outside keeps the text. An emptied text shows the component's again.
  • Picture and link. A click on an open picture or button selects the place and puts the focus in its row under “This place”.
  • Not open. Another element of the component is selected read-only: the panel says whose it is and offers “Edit the component”; its rows are dimmed.
  • “Edit the component”. It is the inline editing of today: the notice is shown once per component, the change reaches every place, “Save” sends the component first.
  • No open field. A component with no open field is selected, edited and drawn exactly as today.

  • Who. A content editor may change the open fields of a place on a page that is theirs, and nothing else of the component. The rows, the dot and the reset are the same as a designer's.
  • “Edit the component”. The button is in the foot only for a person who may edit this component: every designer, and a content editor when the component is opened to content editors (and granted, for a user).

  • The list. Under the small head “Variants”: “Default” first, then the variants in the order they were added, each with the count of what it changes. The ticked row is the one the canvas shows. With no variant the list holds “Default” alone.
  • Adding. “Add a variant” (quiet, small) puts a name field in the list: Enter adds it and starts editing it, Escape drops it. A taken or empty name keeps the field, with “This name is taken.” under it.
  • Editing. Choosing a row shows the component in that variant and starts editing the component (today's notice, once). A line across the top of the canvas names the variant until “Done” or Escape returns to “Default”.
  • What it keeps. Classes and options of the component's nodes, and nothing else. A row the variant changes carries the dot; the tip of its undo arrow is “Reset to the component”.
  • Refused. Adding, removing or moving an element while a variant is edited is refused with the amber line; texts and pictures are the component's and stay so.
  • Deleting. “Delete” needs no question: a place holds only the variant's name and shows the default when the name is gone. The answer is a Result line with “Undo”.
  • Keyboard. Up and Down move in the list, Enter chooses a row, F2 renames, Delete deletes the focused variant (never “Default”).
  • Saving. Variants are saved with the component, by “Save”; nothing is sent by itself, so there is no busy state.

  • The row. “Variant” is the first row of “This place”, a plain list of “Default” and the component's variants. It is not shown for a component with no variant.
  • The dot. A place on a variant other than “Default” carries the dot on the row and counts as a change; the undo arrow returns it to “Default”.
