Hotspots
  • Where: after the choosers, at the right end; “Reset” keeps to its left. The switch is drawn for the default record as for a chosen one, and only for whoever may edit that item on its own screen. For anyone else nothing stands there.
  • Off each time. The record, the visitor and the case are kept for the page; the switch is not: a Preview never opens ready to change a record.
  • On: the label turns green and the frames of R2 appear. A click on a frame edits; a click anywhere else does what it does in the Preview. A framed text inside a link is edited, not followed.
  • The link “Open the record” stands where the switch would be and opens the record’s own screen in a new tab. RE3 draws it where the read says the change would wait for approval; RE4 for a product, an event and any other kind.
  • Another record, visitor or case chosen while it is on: the page is drawn again with its frames. With changes not saved the bar of R4 asks first.
  • Keyboard: Tab reaches the switch after the choosers, Space turns it. Then Tab goes through the frames in the page’s order, Enter edits one, Escape leaves it as it was.

  • Framed: a text, a formatted text, a number, a date and a picture of this item, wherever the page draws it: in the item’s own widget, in the page’s record values, inside a shared component. The list is RE2’s (designer/record-edit-in-place-spots.md).
  • Not framed: the design’s own words (“Tasting notes”, “Add to cart”), the items of a list, another record’s values, an address, a summary cut from a longer text, and a field whose control is none of the five. Those are corrected on the record’s own screen.
  • Drawn over the page: a frame is an outline. It takes no room, moves nothing and is never part of the page that is saved or published.
  • The name is the field’s label as the collection’s form has it, shown above the frame under the pointer and for the focused frame.
  • A field drawn twice (a title in the heading and in the path above it) is framed in both places; typing in one changes the other as it is typed.
  • The dot marks a changed field until it is saved or discarded. A field put back to what the record holds loses it.

  • Text: typed where it stands. Enter or a click outside keeps it, Escape gives back what was there. A line takes no line break, and what is pasted comes in as plain text.
  • Formatted text: a small bar above the frame with Bold, Italic, Link, a bulleted and a numbered list, nothing more. Enter starts a paragraph. What the field holds beyond these (a table, a picture) stays as stored.
  • Number: digits and the decimal mark of the site. The unit beside it is the design’s and is not part of the frame.
  • Date: the designer’s calendar under the frame; the date is shown in the page’s own form. A field of date and time is not one of the five.
  • Picture: “Change picture” opens a small menu. “Choose a picture…” is the designer’s picture chooser; “Alternative text” is there only when the page binds it to a field; “Remove the picture” only when the field may be empty. A replaced file is never deleted.
  • Refused in place: a required field left empty or a number that is none turns the frame red with the reason under it. It is not counted as a change until it is put right; Escape gives back the old value.

This changes the record wherever it is shown.

  • The bar is the designer’s dark chrome, over the page’s foot only while something is changed; the page gets that much room under it and “Hotspots” moves above it. It counts fields, not keystrokes.
  • “Save to the record” writes every changed field at once through the item’s own writer, so the field’s rules, the address rename and the item’s history apply. The page is then drawn again from the saved record.
  • “Discard” gives every field back to what the record holds and asks nothing; the bar leaves.
  • Apart from the design: nothing here is part of Save, Publish or the design’s undo. Ctrl+Z inside a field undoes its typing only. A design edit that refreshes the Preview keeps the changed fields and the open one.
  • Refused by the record: the field turns red with the reason the record’s screen would give; nothing of the save is written, the other changes stay counted.
  • Leaving (the switch turned off, another record chosen, the window closed) with changes not saved: the bar turns amber and asks, with “Keep editing” first. If the item is gone or may no longer be edited, the line under the “Preview as” bar says so and the switch leaves.

This changes the record wherever it is shown.

  • “Edit this record’s content” is a row of the menu of an element bound to a field of a collection item, and of the command palette while such an element is selected. It opens the Preview on that record with the switch on and the frame of that field focused.
  • For another kind of record, and where the person’s change would wait for approval, the row reads “Open the record” and opens the record’s own screen in a new tab. For whoever may not edit the item there is no row.
  • No button is added to the page bar, the canvas bar or a panel’s head (designer/designer-light-and-simple.md). The other rows of the menu are today’s.
  • Narrow: the one button of the bar opens its sheet; “Edit content here” is a row with the switch under the choosers, above “Reset”. Where the link stands in its place, the row is “Open the record”.
  • The foot bar in a narrow window puts its words on the first line and its buttons on the second. The text bar, the calendar and the picture menu open as sheets from the bottom.
  • Touch: a tap on a frame edits it; the field’s name shows for the frame being edited, since nothing hovers.
