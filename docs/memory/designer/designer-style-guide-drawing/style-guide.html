The style guide: what a design is made of, on one sheet

The drawing of the Style guide (designer/designer-style-guide-rules.md): boards G1 to G5 and G7 for step SG2, G6 and G7 for step SG3. The window’s head and strip are the designer’s dark chrome in both pictures; the sheet inside is the sample design “Still Water Yoga” in its own look. The blocks and their rows are the model’s (designer/design-style-guide-model.md); names, colours and counts are examples.

  • The row. “Style guide”, last in the Preview menu under a line, and a row of the command palette. No button in the page bar, the canvas bar or a panel’s head; no key.
  • The window. 1040 px wide at most, 24 px from the window’s edges, as high as the window allows. The head is the designer’s: “Style guide”, the design’s name, “Light | Dark”, “Print or save as PDF”, the close button. Escape closes it; focus returns to the Preview button.
  • The strip names the six blocks in their order. A name scrolls the sheet to its block; the block on screen is the marked one. With the keyboard the strip is one Tab stop and the arrows move over it.
  • The sheet is a frame with the design’s stylesheets and fonts, so every sample is the real thing: the head and the strip are the designer’s dark chrome, the sheet is the design’s. It opens with the design’s name, “Style guide” and the day.
  • Nothing is saved. The sheet is drawn from the design as it is on the canvas at that moment, unsaved changes included; opening it makes no page, file or setting.
  • Colours. A card per colour: its light value at the left and its dark at the right, the name, both values; the designer’s own colours last, marked “Your own”. A design without a dark mode shows one value and no “Light | Dark”.
  • Text on the surface. One row per pair the model measures: the sample, the name, the ratio, and a verdict in words with an icon: “Passes” (4.5 and up), “Large text only” (3 to 4.5), “Too weak for text” (under 3). Never colour alone.
  • Loaded as a part when the row is pressed; while the frame is being drawn the window shows the head and grey lines in the blocks’ places.

  • Type. The two fonts first, then a row per style: a sample sentence in that style at its real size, and at the right its name, its size as the stylesheet writes it, the size in px and the weight. A sample too long for the row is cut with an ellipsis, never wrapped.
  • Which rows. The model’s order: Display 1 to 6, Heading 1 to 6, Lead, Text, Small, then the stylesheet’s own classes. A built-in the stylesheet changes carries the note “changed in the stylesheet”, an own class “your own class”.
  • Scale. “Aa” at each step of the type scale with the step’s name and px, on one base line.
  • Spacing. A bar per size at three times its length: the base unit at 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 units, the section rhythm, then the designer’s own sizes in the accent. The name at the left, the px at the right.
  • A name that is a variable is underlined: it opens that variable (board G6). A style without a variable of its own has a plain name.
  • Sample sentences are the designer’s, translated texts, the same for every design; the design’s own words are not read.

  • Shape. A tile per corner size, the line width and the shadow, each drawn with the value it names; the shadow by its option’s label.
  • Controls. A row per control, a column per state: Rest, Hover, Focus, Off. The states are drawn by the design’s own stylesheet with the state forced, so a missing focus ring shows as missing.
  • The rows: main, second and outline button, link, text field, choice list, tick box, as the model lists them. Under 720 px of sheet the four states stand two by two under the control’s name.
  • Nothing here can be pressed: the samples take no click and no Tab stop; only the underlined names do.
  • A design that styles no outline button still shows the row: it is what a visitor would get.
  • Light and dark: every block follows the “Light | Dark” choice of the head at once (board G5).

  • A card per component, two in a row, sorted by name: the puzzle icon, the name, “on 14 pages”, and under it the component drawn with the design’s stylesheets at the card’s width. System widgets are not listed.
  • A tall component is cut at 360 px with a fade and “Show all of it”, which opens it to its full height in place.
  • Drawn as it is scrolled to. The first screen at once; a card further down shows three grey lines until it comes within one screen of the view. A design with a hundred components opens as fast as one with five.
  • A component that cannot be drawn (it needs a record, or its tree failed to load) says so in one line; its name still opens it.
  • “not placed” where a component is on no page: the count is words, never a button.
  • A design with no shared component shows the block with one line: “This design has no shared component yet.”

  • “Light | Dark” opens on the mode the canvas shows. It changes the sheet only, never the canvas or the design. The pairs are measured for the mode shown.

  • A click on a colour, type or spacing name closes the sheet and opens Settings, Theme, “Variables” with that variable’s fold open and its row marked. The tip under the pointer says “Open in Variables”.
  • A component’s name closes the sheet and opens that component for editing, as its row of the palette’s “Shared” tab does. Its tip: “Open the component”.
  • Names are links: Tab stops in the sheet’s order, Enter opens. A person who may not change the design gets plain names.
  • “Print or save as PDF” opens the browser’s print window on the sheet alone: no head, no strip, every block opened to its full height, every component drawn first. While they are being drawn the button reads “Getting it ready…”.
  • The pages. Every page carries the design’s name, “Style guide” and the day at the top, the site’s address and “1 of 3” at the foot. A block starts on a new page only when less than a third of it fits; a row, a card and a control’s row are never split.
  • Colours are printed as colours (backgrounds on), in the mode the sheet shows; a dark sheet prints dark only when “Dark” is chosen. Underlines of the names are left out on paper.
