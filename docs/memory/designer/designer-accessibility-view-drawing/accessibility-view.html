The accessibility view: the page as a keyboard, a screen reader and another eye meet it

The drawing of plans/designer-accessibility-view.md: boards V1 to V4 and V6 for step AV2, V5 and V6 for step AV3. The canvas, the card and the menus are the designer’s dark chrome in both pictures; the page in the canvas is the sample site “Still Water Yoga”. What is counted and named comes from the reader (designer/accessibility-reader.md); the sentences of faults are the audit’s.

15 stops. One step jumps back on the page.
  • The row. “Accessibility view”, a switch row after the View menu’s other switches, counted in the View button’s badge, and “Show accessibility view” / “Hide accessibility view” in the command palette. No button in the canvas bar, no key. Design mode only.
  • The card. 258 px, in the canvas’s bottom right corner, 12 px from its edges, over the page where there is no room beside it. Its chevron folds it to its head (board V6); its close button turns the view off, as the switch does.
  • One kind of mark at a time. “Tab order”, “Headings” and “Names” are one choice: marks of two kinds over each other cannot be read. Pressing the chosen kind again clears the canvas of marks, so a “See as” filter can be looked at alone.
  • Each row says what it found: the count, and in amber with the warning icon the number of marked places. The numbers are worked out when the view goes on and again when the hand rests after a change.
  • “See as” is beside the three, not one of them: a filter stays on whatever kind is chosen (board V5).
  • The view’s colour is one: fuchsia for every mark, apart from the blue of what is chosen, the green of shared components and the indigo of system widgets. What is marked as wrong is amber and always carries the warning icon or a word.
  • Kept: on or off and the kind chosen are remembered in the browser as the View menu’s other switches are. “See as” is not: it is “Off” whenever the designer opens.
  • Nothing enters the tree or the Preview: the marks are drawn in the canvas’s own layer and take no room on the page.

15 stops. One step jumps back on the page.
  • The number sits on the element’s top left corner, half outside it, 20 px high. The line joins the numbers’ middles in the order of the stops, 1.5 px, under the numbers and over the page.
  • A step that jumps back (the reader’s back and jumps()): the number it arrives at is amber and reads “2 · jumps back”, the line of that step is amber and dashed. Here the field with tabindex 1 is reached first, so the keyboard then jumps up to the logo.
  • A positive tabindex is written on its number (“1 · tabindex 1”), since it is why the order differs from the page’s.
  • Not reached, no mark: a hidden or off element, a closed fold’s content, a negative tabindex. Where a script moves focus the view says nothing.
  • Under the pointer a number grows a ring and the tip names the stop as a screen reader would: “8 · Link “See the timetable””. A click chooses the element; the panel then shows it as any chosen element.
  • The card’s foot says the count and the jumps in one sentence. A jump is marked, not listed as a fault: the audit has no rule for it, and the view finds no fault the audit does not know.

  • The tag “H1” to “H6” stands above the heading’s first line, and the heading is framed. A heading without a level of its own (role="heading") reads “H2 · assumed” and is never marked.
  • A skipped level is amber and dashed and reads “H4 · skips H3”, on the first heading that skips only, as the audit counts it.
  • The outline in the card: a row per heading, indented by its level, cut with an ellipsis. A row chooses its heading and scrolls the canvas to it; the list scrolls inside the card past eight rows.
  • A marked place opens the fault’s card when it is clicked: the audit’s own sentence for that rule, “Fix” where the audit has one, and “Show in the audit”, which opens the audit panel at that row. The click chooses the element too.
  • “Fix” is the audit’s: the same action, the same undo step. The view adds no fix of its own.
  • A page with no heading: no mark, and the card says “This page has no heading.” in the outline’s place.

5 regions, 4 pictures. Three places have no name.
  • A region is outlined with a dashed line and tagged with its role and, where it has one, its name: “Banner”, “Navigation “Main menu””, “Main”, “Form “Newsletter””, “Content info”. The roles are the reader’s; a form or a region without a name is none.
  • A picture carries a chip at its bottom left: its text in quotes, cut at the picture’s width; “decoration” in italics where it is marked as one; amber “no text” where it has none.
  • A control is marked only when it has no name: an amber dashed frame and “Button · no name”, “Link · no name”, “Field · no label”, “Frame · no title”. A named control shows its name as a tip under the pointer.
  • The tags never cover each other: a region’s tag is inside its top left corner, the navigation’s above its frame, “Main” in its top right corner, a control’s under it.
  • The amber places open the fault’s card as on board V3. The card’s row counts them: here a picture, a button and a field.
  • The words are the designer’s, translated; a name is shown as the page has it.

  • The values: Off, Red-weak, Green-weak, Blue-weak, No colour, Blurred. One at a time. The two common ones carry how many people see so, in grey.
  • While a filter is on a pill stands at the foot of the canvas: “Seen as: green-weak” and “Turn off”. Nobody mistakes a filtered canvas for the design.
  • It filters the page only: the canvas’s frame, the marks, the card and every panel keep their colours. It works with any kind of mark, and with none.
  • Style only: a colour matrix or a blur on the canvas’s page; no picture is read, nothing enters the tree, the Preview and the saved design never have it.
  • Never kept: the filter goes off with the view, on a reload and when the designer is opened again.
  • Reduced motion and dark canvas are untouched: the filter is laid over whatever the canvas shows.
