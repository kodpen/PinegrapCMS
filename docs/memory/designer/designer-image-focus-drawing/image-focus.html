Image focus

The sheet of the plan “Image focus”: a point on the picture says what must stay in view when a frame cuts it, on every screen. Sample: a 3:2 photograph on the page “About us”, shown in a wide banner on a desktop and in a tall frame on a phone; the teacher stands right of the middle. Every label is in English as the step writes it.

  • The mode. The frame keeps its place and size; the picture moves under it and cannot be dragged past its own edge. Nothing else on the canvas can be selected until it ends.

On every screen

Every screen
  • Under, never beside: the whole picture takes the control’s width, at most 200 px tall; the frames stand under it. Beside it they were 34 to 104 px wide, too small to judge a cut.
  • Two rows: phone and tablet share the first, desktop has the second. In a row the frames have one height: the largest at which the row fits the control’s width, at most 120 px. The shapes are the ones the control works out today.
  • A name under each frame: its icon and “Phone”, “Tablet”, “Desktop”. The screen the canvas bar shows has the ring and the bright name; a screen’s own focus turns its name blue.
  • One frame: when every screen has the same shape and the same focus (an Image with an aspect ratio and no screen’s own value), the three would be the same picture: one frame “Every screen”, at most 140 px tall.
  • What stays: the point and its keys, the value read out, “Reset”, the chip, the foot lines, “Same on every screen”, a typed value, a bound card, what is written. Only where the frames stand and how large they are changes.
  • Narrow: the same rule by the control’s width. In the Options panel as a sheet on a phone the rows reach the 120 px cap and stand at the left.
