  • The head counts the sections and says where they came from. The amber pill counts the rows the reader was unsure of and goes when each has had its kind chosen.
  • A row: the handle, the kind (a list, F4), the heading with the beginning of its words, how much it holds, the arrow that opens the words, the cross that leaves it out.
  • An unsure row carries the amber mark and, in place of its words, the reason in a sentence. The reader’s best reading is already chosen: “Build the page” never waits for a mark.
  • “Build the page” in the New design window leads to the start questions, then the design opens on the page. From an empty page the sections are put on the page at once, as one step of undo.
  • “Back” returns to the form with the text or the file kept. Reading again throws the rows’ changes away, and says so first.
  • “Sort it for me” is drawn only where Pinegrap AI is on (F5, step FD4). FD3 builds the rows without it.

  • The list of an unsure row opens on “It could be”: the reader’s two readings, its own ticked, then “Something else” with the other kinds the section’s words can fill. A sure row’s list has no captions.
  • Choosing a kind, the ticked one too, takes the row’s mark away and puts its words back in the row. A kind the words cannot fill (a table without rows) is not in the list.
  • Opened, a row shows its heading and its items as fields, one line each, in the kind’s shape: a card’s title and text, a question and its answer, a figure and its label. Only one row is open at a time.
  • Left out: the cross strikes the heading and turns into “Put back”; the row keeps its place and the head counts “8 of 9”. Nothing is deleted before the page is built.
  • Moved: the handle is dragged and a line shows where the row lands. With the keyboard the handle takes the focus and the up and down arrows move the row; the new place is read out.
  • Pictures and tables open as what they are: a picture with its alternative text as the one field, a table as its cells.

Try again

Pinegrap AI is off, or the person may not use it: the head has no button, and nothing else on the rows changes.

9 sectionsread from brief.docx · 1,240 words2 to check
  • A proposal is a line under its row, tinted, with its own two answers. The row above it stays as it is until one is pressed; then the line goes.
  • A kind: “Accept” changes the row’s kind and takes its mark away; “Leave” only removes the line. Proposals come for unsure rows first, and for a sure row only when the words clearly fit another kind.
  • A shorter text comes only for a passage over 300 words. “Show” opens yours beside the proposed one; “Use the shorter text” replaces the section’s words, “Keep mine” leaves them. There is no button that accepts all texts.
  • The words are the person’s. A proposed text is never put in by itself, and the page built without a press on “Use the shorter text” carries the document’s words unchanged.
  • While it works the button waits and shows “Sorting…”; the rows stay usable, and a row changed meanwhile keeps the person’s choice over the proposal.
  • The button’s line says what leaves the site, before the first press. The press is counted as any Pinegrap AI action of the designer is.

  • Reading a file shows its name; a paste is read at once and shows no wait. “Cancel” stops the reading and closes the window.
  • A refusal is a red line above the form, in the words drawn here; the box and the drop place stay so the next try needs no step back.
  • Cut short: the amber line gives the count and the share that was read, in the reader’s own figures. The rows are those of the part that was read.
  • Pictures of a document go to the file manager when the page is built, each to its place in its section. The blue line counts them, and those too large to take.
  • No headings: the text is one row of the kind “Text”, with the line that says how a heading is recognised. It can be built as it is.
  • At 390 px the window fills the screen and the starts are a strip. A row puts its kind and its buttons on one line and its words under them; the foot’s buttons stack, the filled one first.
