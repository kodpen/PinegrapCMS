  • The window is History’s Compare window: its head, its tabs, its foot. The tab “Preview” becomes “Look” and stands first; “Code” is the markup tab as it is today. The two frames of “Preview” are the Look tab’s “Side by side”.
  • The line over the pictures: the left side’s name, “Slider” / “Side by side”, “Desktop” (1280 px) / “Phone” (390 px), the right side’s name. Each name says in grey what it is: “since 12 Nov, 14:02”, “as it is in the designer now”.
  • The slider: both pictures are the whole page, one over the other, scaled to the room. The handle is a white line with a round grip; left of it the page on the site, right of it the draft. It starts in the middle.
  • The handle is dragged, or moved with the arrow keys once it has focus (a ring round the grip); Home and End put it at an end. A click on a picture puts it there.
  • Scrolling moves both pictures as one. Where one page is shorter, the canvas’s backdrop shows under its end.
  • The foot: the count in one sentence, and “Close”. Escape and a click outside close it as today.

  • Button “Book a class”
  • A row is the reader’s sentence with the kind’s icon and, in grey, its place. The kind is said by the words, never by a colour. The count beside “What changed” is the number of rows.
  • A row chosen scrolls the pictures to the place and outlines it in blue with the row’s sentence as its tag. Each picture is scrolled to its own place of the element, so it stands at the same height in both.

  • A row of a text opens when chosen and shows the old and the new words under “Before” and “Now”, as plain text, cut after six lines.
  • A row of several elements (“Look changed (3 elements)”, “Other changes (1)”) outlines them all and opens into their names; a name goes to its element.
  • Nothing is left out: a change the reader cannot name is counted under “Other changes”. A row whose element is not found in a picture still shows in the list and outlines nothing.
